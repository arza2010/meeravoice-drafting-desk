# LinkedIn posts in Meera's voice

## Audience on LinkedIn
Two readers at once, and the post should reward both:
- **Informed consumers** who follow her for formulation literacy (same people as the newsletter).
- **Industry and ecosystem readers**: other D2C founders, formulators, contract manufacturers, retail buyers, investors, potential hires. They care about *operating* insight — what she asked the manufacturer, what the returns data showed, what the testing costs.

Her strongest published post for LinkedIn specifically (India climate / returns data) works because it speaks to the second group ("If you're building a skincare brand for the Indian market, the first question to ask your contract manufacturer is…") while still teaching the first. Prefer that dual-audience angle on LinkedIn; pure consumer explainers belong more naturally in the newsletter.

## The structure (observed across all four published posts)

1. **Concrete hook, in the first ~140 characters** (mobile "see more" cut-off; ~210 on desktop). One of four types she uses:
   - *Label claim + reversal*: "The niacinamide serum you're using probably has the ingredient listed at 5% or 10% on the label. That number is almost certainly meaningless…"
   - *Dated scene*: "In 2021 I was sitting in a stability review meeting…"
   - *Hard data*: "In the 12 months to June 2025, 23% of our product returns…"
   - *Observed industry moment*: "Last September I was at a trade fair in Mumbai. One of the booths had a banner that said…"
   The hook must contain a number, a place, a date, or a verbatim label claim. Never a generic question or aphorism.
2. **The turn** (1–2 sentences): what's actually going on beneath the surface. Often the three-beat deflation.
3. **Mechanism, walked through in prose sequence**: "The first thing… The second thing the percentage doesn't tell you is… The third thing is…" Three parts is her default.
4. **Bounding statement**: "I want to be precise about what I'm not saying here."
5. **Skinstinct as practice, briefly, near the end**: what they require, document, or got wrong. Never more than ~20% of the post. Never framed as a pitch.
6. **Reader test + close**: what to ask, and how to read the answer. Last line is short and flat.

## Length
- Her published posts: ~2,450–3,340 characters (426–537 words). One (the niacinamide post) is **over LinkedIn's 3,000-character hard limit** and would not publish as written.
- 2026 benchmarks put the engagement sweet spot around 1,200–2,000 characters, with diminishing returns beyond ~2,000.
- **Default target: 1,500–2,200 characters.** Allowed up to 2,800 for a flagship deep-dive where every paragraph carries a new fact. Always count characters and report the count.
- To get there without losing voice: cut the second example in any pair, compress the Skinstinct paragraph to two sentences, drop the recap sentence before the close. Keep the numbers, the bounding line, and the reader test — those are the voice.

## Formatting for the feed
Her text is dense prose paragraphs. On LinkedIn, keep her prose but break into short paragraphs of 1–3 sentences with a blank line between, so it reads on mobile. Isolate the three-beat deflation as its own paragraph. Still no bullets, bold, emojis, or hashtags — zero hashtags is on-voice and benchmarks show no penalty for none (only for excess).

## Comments
LinkedIn weights early comments heavily, and her published posts never invite one. An on-voice way to invite comments without bait is a genuine, specific request for reader data at the end, e.g. "If you've asked a brand for the pH of a product, I'd be interested in what they told you." Use at most one such line, and only when it's a question she'd actually want answered.

## Repurposing a newsletter into a LinkedIn post
Keep the one mechanism with the sharpest number. Replace "Hi," and the meta-opening with a concrete hook from the list above. Drop the sign-off. Add the industry/operator angle if one exists. Target ~1,800 characters.

## Skeleton (for orientation, not to copy)
```
[Hook: specific label claim / dated scene / data point — <140 chars]
[Turn: what that hides. Optional three-beat deflation.]

[Mechanism part one.]

[Part two.]

[Part three.]

[Bounding: "I'm not saying X. I'm saying Y."]

[Skinstinct practice or admission — 1–2 sentences.]

[Reader test: ask this, in writing. If the answer is vague, that's useful information.]
```
