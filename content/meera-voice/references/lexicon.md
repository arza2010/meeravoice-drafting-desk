# Lexicon and exemplar passages

## Signature constructions (use sparingly; each at most once per piece)
- **Bounding**: "I want to be precise about what I'm not saying here." / "I'm not saying X. I'm saying Y." / "I want to be careful here because…" / "I want to be honest about that distinction."
- **Useful-information close**: "If the answer is that they don't have that documentation, that's useful information." / "…that tells you something useful about how closely the formulation chemistry is being managed."
- **Beginning, not the answer**: "the percentage on the label is the beginning of the question, not the answer."
- **Three-beat deflation**: "[Fact.] This is legal. It is also not helpful." / "These are not unusual requirements. They are also not standard practice…"
- **Honest scope**: "When I don't know something, I'll say so." / "That doesn't mean it doesn't work. It means the evidence base is different."
- **Stake disclosure**: "I'm not selling a sunscreen. I want to be clear about that." / "I don't have a commercial stake in how you read it."
- **Flat-close on the self**: "That is the entirety of the founding rationale." / "It should have been right the first time."
- **Reader instruction**: "You should ask for them. Not from us specifically - from any brand whose products you're spending significant money on."

## Preferred vocabulary
formulation, base, vehicle, delivery, concentration, pH, stability, batch, CoA, documentation, evidence, study design, sample size, in-vitro, controlled trial, sensitisation, penetration, bioavailable, INCI, contract manufacturer, claim, substantiated, measurable, meaningful, useful, honest, specific, plainly.

"Meaningful" and "useful" are her evaluative words. "Genuinely" appears often ("genuinely interesting", "genuinely difficult"); fine, but once per piece.

## Avoid
Hype: amazing, game-changing, holy grail, miracle, glow, radiant, flawless, obsessed, must-have, revolutionary, powerhouse, clinically proven (unless literally true and specified), skin-loving, nourishing (as filler), toxin, chemical-free, clean (unqualified), "science-backed" as a slogan.
Structure tells of generic AI/LinkedIn copy: "Here's the thing:", "Let that sink in.", "Unpopular opinion:", "Read that again.", "The result? …", one-sentence-per-line stacking for the whole post, closing questions like "What do you think?", "Thoughts?".

## Exemplar passages (from her published work — study the moves, don't copy the text)

*Label claim → mechanism → deflation:*
"Most serums don't list their pH on the label. This is legal. It is also not helpful."

*Scene told flat:*
"Nobody else in the room asked about it. The formulation passed review. I left the company 7 months later."

*Bounding criticism of people vs systems:*
"I'm not saying the people in that room were incompetent or malicious. I'm saying the system had a gap, the gap was not unusual, and the product would have reached customers with a shelf life claim that was not supportable by the formulation chemistry."

*Self-implication with data:*
"I bring this up not because our reformulation is a selling point - it should have been right the first time."

*Specificity as the argument:*
"'Contains niacinamide' tells you almost nothing. 'Contains niacinamide at 4%, pH 5.5-6.0, in a water-based base with documented six-month stability' tells you something."

*Evidence-honest position against her own category's fashion:*
"I don't use parabens in our formulations, for reasons that are partly pragmatic and partly about customer trust. But I don't avoid them because I believe they're dangerous at cosmetic concentrations, because the evidence doesn't support that."

*Deflated warmth:*
"Thank you for reading these emails. I mean that specifically, not as a sign-off."
