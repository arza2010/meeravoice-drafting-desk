# Newsletters in Meera's voice

## The contract with the reader
Newsletter 001 set it explicitly, and every issue must honour it: this list is not where she sells. Product emails happen only at launch, and even then explain formulation choices. Most issues are formulation science written plainly. She says when she doesn't know, when the research is inconclusive, and when the industry is misrepresenting something.

Readers are mostly customers and people referred by customers: urban Indian skincare users who read labels, many with sensitive or reactive skin, many in humid cities. They're curious, a bit skeptical, and short on reliable sources.

## Structure (observed across 11 issues)

1. **Subject line.** Plain, specific, often with a candid aside. Patterns:
   - Topic + candour: "The Vitamin C email (I've been putting this off)"
   - Topic + what's missing: "pH - the question nobody puts on the label"
   - Direct instruction: "The one thing I want you to stop doing with SPF"
   - Balanced verdict: "Clean beauty gets some things right. The rest is making things harder."
   - Honest ledger: "18 months of Skinstinct. Some data, some honesty."
   No emojis, no caps, no urgency, no "you won't believe". Under ~70 characters where possible.
2. **"Hi,"** on its own line. Always.
3. **Opening paragraph: why this, why now.** Usually meta and candid: a reader question she received, a topic she's been avoiding, a warning that she'll be careful. ("I want to be careful writing this one because I've seen this conversation go badly in both directions.")
4. **Plain definition.** What the thing actually is, stripped of marketing ("Peptides are short chains of amino acids.").
5. **Mechanism in stages.** Often three: penetration / concentration / stability; or pH / delivery base / batch. Written as prose sequence with transitions ("Take penetration." "Then there's concentration." "Finally, stability.").
6. **Where the industry blurs it.** The specific gap between label language and formulation reality.
7. **Bounding statement.** "I'm not trying to make you distrust every product on your shelf."
8. **Skinstinct disclosure.** Either documented practice or "we don't sell this" / "we haven't solved this yet".
9. **Practical takeaway.** What to look for or ask, concretely ("apply the Vitamin C first and give it five to ten minutes before the serum").
10. **Short closing line**, often a promise of a follow-up or a flat statement of values.
11. **"Meera"** on its own line. No title, no P.S. (unless the user asks), no links unless requested.

## Length
Her range is 354–558 words; nearly all sit at 420–500. **Default target: 400–520 words.** This is appropriate for a read-in-inbox educational newsletter: benchmark data shows beauty has among the lowest click-through rates of any category, and her issues contain no links at all, so success is measured by reading and replies, not clicks. Don't pad to fill a range, and don't add CTAs to "improve CTR" unless asked.

## Series logic
She links issues ("More on specific actives in the next few emails"). When writing a series, carry a light thread: reference the previous issue in one clause, promise the next in one line.

## Founder-story issues
Format flips: open with the reluctance to write a celebratory piece, then give (a) the number she's proudest of with honest uncertainty about causation, (b) the thing they got wrong, (c) the thing she didn't expect, (d) what's coming without details, (e) a deflated-but-warm close and a sincere thank-you ("I mean that specifically, not as a sign-off").

## Product-launch issues (none published yet — use this when asked)
Keep the contract: lead with the formulation problem the product solves, give pH, concentrations, packaging rationale, stability protocol, what testing has and hasn't been done (sample size, study type, who ran it), what the product is *not* for. Mention price and availability once, late, plainly.
